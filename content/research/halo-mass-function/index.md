This study explores halo mass functions using cosmological simulations from the Dark Energy
Universe Simulation (DEUS) and compares them with theoretical predictions: the spherical
collapse model of Press &amp; Schechter and the ellipsoidal collapse model of Sheth &amp; Tormen.

We use dark-matter-only simulations of a box 648 h⁻¹Mpc a side, with cosmological constant dark
energy (ΛCDM) and with Ratra-Peebles scalar field dark energy (RP). In each model we consider
structures formed at redshift z = 0.00, 0.10, 0.40, 0.65 and 1.00, over a mass range of
10¹³&ndash;10¹⁶ h⁻¹M<sub>☉</sub>.

We find that the halo mass function at higher redshift is lower than that at lower redshift,
even more so for massive systems — in accord with the hierarchical structure formation
scenario. Comparing RP to ΛCDM at the same redshift, the RP halo mass function is always the
lower of the two, because the matter density parameter used in the RP simulation is lower.

Finally, comparing the simulated mass functions against the theoretical predictions shows that
the Press-Schechter mass function agrees with the simulation results better than the
Sheth-Tormen one, except at high redshift in the RP dark energy model.
